Nunc Is Coming to iPhone and iPad

Sep 29, 2026
FeatureiOSTimeline

Coming soon: Nunc on iPhone and iPad shows today as a calendar-style day view, with calendar events, reminders, RSS posts, and plugin events placed on one hourly grid.

In an upcoming release, Nunc is coming to iPhone and iPad, and it looks different from the Mac app. Instead of a screen-edge timeline, it shows today as a calendar-style day view: an hourly grid like the day view in a calendar app. Calendar events, reminders, RSS posts, and plugin events all go on that one grid. Requires iOS 26 or iPadOS 26.

A calendar-style day view

On Mac, Nunc lives as a screen-edge timeline or a Desktop Event Wall. On iPhone, the app opens to today’s day view. Meetings and reminders appear as blocks at their scheduled time, and RSS headlines appear at the time they were published, each in its source’s color. All-day events sit in a strip above the hours; when there are several, tap the strip to expand it. A red line marks the current time.

Busy hours fill up fast, especially with news feeds. The screenshot above is at the default, most compact spacing, so a busy afternoon like 14:00 to 16:00 gets crowded and titles are cut short. Spread two fingers to widen the hour spacing, up to 16 times, with half-hour, quarter-hour, and finer marks appearing as you zoom in. Point-in-time items such as headlines and reminders keep their height, so crowded ones move apart, while timed events stretch with their duration. When items overlap, Nunc places them side by side only if their titles would collide; otherwise the later one stacks slightly indented on top of the longer event.

On first launch, a short welcome walks through zooming, installing plugins, and setting up rules with small interactive demos. You can reopen it anytime from Settings → Getting Started.

Tap to open

On Mac, hovering over an event shows a bubble. On iPhone, tap an event to open a details sheet: the title and time, where it came from (for example BBC News · RSS Reader), the notes or article summary, the image, and Open Original for the source link. Reminders also show whether they are completed.

Reading in Safari’s view

On Mac, Nunc ships its own Reader Mode. On iPhone and iPad, we decided not to build another one. Links open in the system’s Safari view inside Nunc. It switches to Reader automatically when a page supports it, and Safari’s page translation is built in.

This means articles read and translate the same way they do in Safari, and those features improve with system updates instead of waiting for a Nunc release. If you would rather skip the details sheet, turn on Open Links in App in Settings, and tapping an event with a web link goes straight to the page.

Translated titles

Turn on Translate Titles in Settings to read headlines in your own language right on the timeline. Translation runs on-device with the system’s translation, no internet needed, or through the AI model you configure in Settings. The timeline shows only the translation, and the details sheet keeps the original title. If a language pair is not on your device yet, Settings offers to download it. Rules match both the translated and the original title.

On iPad

On iPad, Nunc runs in any orientation, in Split View, and in Stage Manager. In a wide window, event details open in a sidebar next to the day view instead of a sheet. With a keyboard, press Command-R to refresh, Command-Comma to open Settings, and Esc to close. With a pointer, events highlight on hover, and a right-click offers Copy Title and Open Original.

Plugins and rules

Plugins come from the same Plugin Market: install one, fill in its parameters, and turn it on. Rules can hide, pin, recolor, add symbols, replace text, and highlight text, and can apply only on certain days of the week or in light or dark appearance.

Rules and templates written on Mac can be brought over as a .sidescript file with Import SideScript, and Export SideScript writes your rules back in the same format. Rules you already have are not added twice, entries that cannot be read are skipped, and Nunc tells you how many were imported, already existed, or were skipped.

The app does not include a plugin script editor or a SideScript editor. Syncing across devices through iCloud is not available yet, so sources, feeds, and plugins are set up separately on each device.

Rolling out in an upcoming Nunc release—stay tuned.
